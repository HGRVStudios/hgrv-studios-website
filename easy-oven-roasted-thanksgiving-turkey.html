Easy Oven-Roasted Thanksgiving Turkey

6–8 servings | 10–12 lb turkey | Oven: 325°F | Cook to 165°F in breast, thigh, and wing
Ingredients · Before You Start · Instructions · Storage

A clear, beginner-friendly guide to a golden, herb-butter turkey, with practical explanations right where you need them.

Difficulty: Beginner • Total time: About 3–4 hours of roasting, plus resting

Equipment: Roasting pan and rack, meat thermometer, mixing bowl, spoon or spatula, carving knife and cutting board.

Cooking times are estimates, not guarantees. The turkey is ready only when the thickest breast, innermost thigh, and innermost wing each reach 165°F.

Ingredients

IngredientAmount
Turkey1 whole turkey, 10–12 lb, completely thawed
Butter6 tbsp unsalted butter, softened
Olive oil1 tbsp
Fine sea salt2 tsp ONLY for a turkey without added salt; see salted-butter note in Step 3
Black pepper1 tsp
Garlic powder1 tsp
Dried thyme leaves1 tsp
Dried rosemary, crushed½ tsp
Paprika½ tsp
Onion1 large, cut into wedges
Carrots2 medium, cut into large chunks
Celery2 stalks, cut into large pieces
Low-sodium broth1 cup chicken or turkey broth; water works too
Optional cavity aromatics1 lemon, halved; 3–4 fresh thyme sprigs; 2 fresh rosemary sprigs

A sprig is a short stem of a fresh herb with leaves attached. If you cannot find fresh thyme or rosemary for the optional cavity aromatics, simply omit them: the dried herbs in the butter already provide flavor. For an extra herbal note, add up to ¼ tsp more dried thyme to the butter in Step 3 instead. Do not pack loose dried herbs into the cavity.

Before You Start and Equipment

A whole turkey looks intimidating, but the process is manageable: thaw it safely, prepare the pan, spread on seasoned butter, roast, and check the temperature. This version keeps bread stuffing in a separate baking dish so both foods are easier to cook safely.

Thawing and package checks

For a frozen 10–12 lb turkey, allow about 3 days in a refrigerator at 40°F or below (roughly 24 hours for every 4–5 lb). Keep it wrapped on a tray on the bottom shelf. Once thawed in the refrigerator, cook within 1–2 days. Never thaw it on the counter.

For faster thawing, keep the turkey in leakproof packaging, submerge in cold tap water, change the water every 30 minutes, and allow about 30 minutes per pound. Cook immediately after cold-water thawing.

Read the label before salting. A self-basting, pre-brined, or “contains a solution” turkey may already contain salt. Omit the added salt below for a salted or pre-brined bird unless its packaging specifically directs otherwise.

Roasting pan; roasting rack (or a sturdy bed of large vegetables); food thermometer; paper towels; small bowl and fork; knife; cutting board; heat-safe container and strainer for drippings; foil if browning too quickly.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1. Set up the oven and pan

Move an oven rack to the lower third and preheat to 325°F. Cut 1 large onion into wedges, 2 medium carrots into large chunks, and 2 celery stalks into large pieces. Spread them in the roasting pan and set the rack over them. Pour 1 cup low-sodium chicken or turkey broth into the bottom of the pan.

No broth? Use 1 cup water. No roasting rack? A stable bed of large vegetable chunks can raise the turkey, but make sure the bird sits securely above the liquid.

2. Unwrap and dry the turkey

Put the fully thawed 10–12 lb turkey on a large rimmed tray. Remove the packaging. Check BOTH the main body cavity and the neck opening for the neck and giblet packet; remove them. Follow the package instructions for any leg holder or pop-up device. Do not wash the turkey: splashing raw poultry juices spreads germs. Pat the skin dry with paper towels, discard them, and wash hands and contacted surfaces thoroughly.

Why dry it? Less moisture on the skin helps it brown. Keep raw poultry and its juices away from ready-to-eat foods.

3. Mix the seasoned butter

In a small bowl, mix 6 tbsp softened unsalted butter, 1 tbsp olive oil, 2 tsp fine sea salt (ONLY if the turkey has no added salt), 1 tsp black pepper, 1 tsp garlic powder, 1 tsp dried thyme leaves, ½ tsp crushed dried rosemary, and ½ tsp paprika. Stir with a fork until evenly combined. Butter should be spreadable, not melted.

Only have salted butter? Use 6 tbsp salted butter and start with 1 tsp added fine sea salt instead of 2 tsp for a turkey with no added salt. Salt levels vary by brand, so this is a cautious starting adjustment. If the turkey is pre-brined, self-basting, or contains added salt, OMIT the added salt entirely, whether using salted or unsalted butter.

Make it your own: Add ½ tsp dried sage for a classic holiday flavor, ½ tsp more garlic powder for a stronger garlic note, or use 1 tsp paprika total for more color. Smoked paprika changes the flavor noticeably.

4. Loosen the breast skin and apply butter

Place the turkey breast-side up on the rack. Near the neck opening, gently slide clean fingers between the breast skin and the meat, moving slowly to make a pocket without tearing the skin. Spread about HALF of the seasoned butter mixture (roughly half) beneath the skin over the breast meat. Rub the remaining HALF (roughly half) across the outside of the breast, legs, and other exposed skin. Small patches are fine if cold skin makes spreading difficult.

5. Add optional aromatics

If using them, loosely place 1 halved lemon, 3–4 fresh thyme sprigs, and 2 fresh rosemary sprigs in the empty body cavity. Do not pack tightly. Tuck wing tips underneath if possible. Do not add bread stuffing inside the turkey; bake stuffing separately and check its center reaches 165°F.

6. Roast and monitor

Roast the turkey breast-side up, uncovered, in the preheated 325°F oven. A 10–12 lb unstuffed bird often takes roughly 2¾–3¾ hours, but ovens and birds differ. Begin checking temperature around 2½ hours rather than waiting for the timer to finish. Regular basting is unnecessary and repeatedly opening the oven can slow cooking.

If the skin gets too dark before the turkey is done, loosely tent the darkening areas with foil. If the pan juices start scorching, carefully pour about ½ cup warm water or low-sodium broth into the PAN, not over the skin; repeat only if necessary.

7. Check doneness in three places

Insert a food thermometer into the thickest part of the breast, the innermost thigh, and the innermost wing, without touching bone. ALL THREE locations must reach at least 165°F. If one area is below 165°F, continue roasting; check again in about 10–15 minutes as it nears completion. If the breast is done before the thigh, loosely shield the breast with foil while the rest finishes.

The timer tells you WHEN TO CHECK. The thermometer tells you WHEN IT IS SAFE. Do not rely on skin color or a pop-up timer alone.

8. Rest the turkey

When all three locations reach 165°F, carefully transfer the turkey to a clean board or platter. Rest about 30 minutes before carving. Loosely tent with foil if desired. This makes carving easier and reduces juices lost onto the board.

9. Save the drippings for gravy

Carefully strain the liquid from the roasting pan into a heat-safe container. Let it settle briefly, then separate the fat from the juices. Reserve both if your gravy recipe calls for them. Keep drippings hot or refrigerate promptly; treat them as perishable food.

10. Carve and serve

10.1 Remove the legs and thighs

After the turkey has rested, set it breast-side up on a sturdy cutting board. Pull one leg gently away from the body to reveal the seam between the thigh and breast. Cut through the skin along that seam, then bend the leg outward until you can locate the hip joint. Cut through the joint, not through the bone, to remove the leg-and-thigh section. Repeat on the other side.

10.2 Separate each drumstick from its thigh

Place a removed leg-and-thigh section skin-side down. Bend the drumstick to find the knee joint, then cut through the joint to separate the drumstick and thigh. Repeat for the other leg. Serve the drumsticks whole; slice or pull the thigh meat from the bone if desired.

10.3 Remove the breast meat

Find the raised breastbone at the center of the turkey. Starting at the top, cut down along one side of the breastbone, keeping the knife close to the ribs. Gently lift the breast as you follow the rib cage until the entire breast comes free. Repeat on the opposite side.

10.4 Slice the breast across the grain

Place each removed breast on the board and look for the direction of its muscle fibers, called the grain. Cut across those fibers, not parallel to them, into roughly ¼–½-inch slices. Keep the slices together as you transfer them to a platter.

10.5 Remove the wings

Pull one wing away from the body to locate the shoulder joint. Bend it back gently to expose the joint, then cut between the bones through the joint. Repeat on the other side. Avoid trying to chop through solid bone.

10.6 Collect the remaining meat

Check along the rib cage, back, and around the joints for pieces of meat still attached to the carcass. Carefully pull or cut them free, watching for small bones and cartilage. Add these pieces to the serving platter or refrigerate them promptly for another meal.

10.7 Arrange the serving platter

Arrange sliced breast meat in the center of a clean platter. Add the thigh meat alongside it, then position drumsticks and wings around the edges. Include any remaining pieces of meat and serve with warm gravy.

10.8 Serve the carved turkey

Bring the platter to the table once the turkey and sides are ready. If the carved turkey will not be served promptly, follow the hot-holding or refrigerating-and-reheating instructions below rather than leaving it at room temperature.

Make-Ahead, Holding, Storage, and Reheating

If the turkey is ready early, you can keep it hot in an oven with a Keep Warm setting ONLY if the turkey itself stays at 140°F or above. An oven’s “warm” label does not prove the food is safely hot: check the meat periodically with a thermometer. If your oven has no Keep Warm setting, its lowest setting may work, but again verify the FOOD stays at least 140°F. Long hot holding can dry the meat and soften the skin; keep delays as short as practical.

If you cannot reliably maintain 140°F or above, do not leave the turkey on the counter. Carve it and refrigerate in shallow containers within 2 hours, then reheat to 165°F before serving. The 2-hour limit also applies to food left at room temperature after cooking.

Within 2 hours, remove leftover meat from the bones and refrigerate in shallow containers at 40°F or below. Use refrigerated leftovers within 3–4 days or freeze promptly for longer storage. Reheat leftovers to 165°F.

For moist reheating, place sliced turkey in a covered baking dish with roughly ¼ cup broth per pound of turkey. Heat in an oven set to at least 325°F until the meat itself reaches 165°F. The time depends on quantity and dish depth.
